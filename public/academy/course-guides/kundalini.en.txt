KUNDALINI REIKI — COMPLETE THREE-LEVEL STUDY GUIDE
=======================================================

A clear study path for self-practice, guided meditation, the symbolic seven-chakra model and teacher-led initiation. All instructions are educational, contemplative and optional. Personal results are not guaranteed.

Level 1. Foundation and Self-Practice
Focus: Learning to observe attention, breathing and bodily sensations, using the language of Reiki and Kundalini as a reflective framework.
- Introduction to the traditional seven-chakra map, the root-to-crown central channel and its symbolic meaning.
- Gassho: gently bring the palms together, choose one intention, sit comfortably and notice your natural breathing.
- Learn optional self-hand placements, grounding and short periods of quiet self-reflection.
- Keep a practice journal: notice your mood, focus, sensations and questions without interpreting them as proof of energy transfer.
- Learn respectful partner work: always request consent, agree on boundaries and make touch optional. A remote visualisation is an intention exercise, not a medical intervention.
Suggested practice: Sit or lie comfortably; place palms together and name one personal intention. Observe breathing and physical sensations for 10–15 minutes, then write three factual observations.
Practice assignment: Repeat the meditation over several days, recording experiences without promising specific outcomes.

Level 2. Kundalini Imagery and Balance
Focus: Working more consciously with concentration, the symbolic Flame of Kundalini and the historical Balance hand gesture.
- Explore the upward earth-to-crown image and reflect on the meaning of the seven chakras as a teaching metaphor.
- Distinguish physical sensations from images, interpretations and expectations.
- Balance: bring corresponding fingertips of both hands together for around 30 seconds while silently naming the intention to return to balance.
- Check in with the whole body after the gesture. Pause if uncomfortable and keep the practice gentle.
- Learn how to return to everyday life through small moments of attention rather than claiming changes in health or external events.
Suggested practice: Compare a simple grounding meditation with the Balance exercise; describe what you actually noticed, without assuming a cause.
Practice assignment: Write down similarities and differences between the two practices, including questions for a teacher.

Level 3. Master and Traditional Extensions
Focus: The teacher-led attunement model, the ethics of passing a spiritual teaching on, and the named symbolic practices in this tradition.
- A Master initiation/attunement is a teacher-led ritual within this spiritual educational system. Ask the teacher to clarify readiness, consent and timing.
- Diamond Reiki: a traditional symbol of clarity and focused attention.
- Crystal Reiki: contemplative imagery and symbolic focus using crystals.
- DNA Reiki: a historical ritual name only; no suggestion that DNA is changed or repaired.
- Birth Trauma Reiki: a historical label, not psychological trauma treatment.
- Location Reiki: visualisation of a place and its personal symbolic meaning.
- Past Life Reiki: imagery and reflection, not proof that past lives can be verified or treated.
- Historical eight-day reflection sequence: Crystal Reiki on days 1–2; DNA Reiki day 3; Birth Trauma day 4; Location day 5; Past Life days 6–8. These are source tradition names and optional historical exercises, not clinical protocols.
Suggested practice: Write a personal code of practice covering respect, informed consent, optional touch, boundaries, honest observation and referral to appropriate health professionals when relevant.
Practice assignment: Present a responsible outline of how you would describe a symbolic practice to another adult.

Everyday Practices
- Start with yourself: choose comfortable, regular, short meditations; pause if they cause distress.
- Intention and hands: palms together or gentle self-touch are optional anchors for attention.
- Partner practice: obtain clear consent before any interaction and do not assume that distance visualisation affects others.
- Reflection on situations: use relationships, spaces and personal goals as themes for self-inquiry without promising external change.
- Learning journal: record what you did, how you felt, and the questions you wish to raise with a teacher.
