# Holistic House — My Course v2: 14-day skills & flexible-beliefs workbook

**Document status:** implemented specification, 2026-10-13 · **Scope:** EN/RU, authenticated client cabinet, self-guided psychoeducation
**Predecessor:** `docs/myspace/holistic-house-my-course-14-day-passports-2026-10-13.md` (v1)
**Canonical target:** `andylitvinov-design/books`, `codex/public-book-library`; page `/{locale}/app/course`
**Content:** original Holistic House texts; psychotherapy-*informed* workbook structure, **not a psychotherapy service**.

## 1. Conceptual upgrade: from 14 meditations to a skill learning laboratory

The v1 curriculum had 84 distinct bilingual lessons (14 days × 6 approved tracks), an authentic 14-day map, daily check-in, brief guidance, action, reflection and optional weekly reviews. However, offering motivation or meditation alone does not teach a new response pattern or flexible belief. v2 adds an **evidence-informed learning loop** every day:

**Notice → Understand → Try a more workable response → Test it safely → Reflect → Repeat the helpful part.**

A 'new belief' is a **plausible, personally chosen working position**, *never* a forced affirmation or a guarantee that a subconscious belief has been 'reprogrammed'. The user may simply notice an automatic thought (ACT-like unhooking) without adopting a replacement thought if that feels more suitable. Deep trauma processing, severe mental-health crises and graded exposure plans are **not** automatically assigned by this self-guided course.

### Scientific and workbook inspirations (inspiration, not clinical equivalence)

| Inspiration | Mechanism contributed | Course placement | Primary source |
|---|---|---|---|

| ACT-informed WHO stress guide | Orienting to present context, unhooking from thoughts, allowing emotion, values-based small action, kindness | Days 4–5, 8, 11, 13 | [WHO Doing What Matters](https://tdr.who.int/home/our-work/global-engagement/9789240003927) |
| DBT-informed skills education | Attention, distress tolerance, emotion regulation and interpersonal effectiveness; pick an acceptable skill rather than demand a feeling | Days 4, 6, 9, 11 | [VA DBT skills overview](https://www.mirecc.va.gov/mirecc/visn16/dbt.asp) |
| WOOP / implementation intentions | Define a realistic wish/goal, notice a likely obstacle, pair a recognizable trigger with a tiny response in an if–then plan | Days 1, 9, 12–14 | [WOOP written practice](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice), [2024 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563) |

**Evidence boundary:** These sources support elements of the approach, not the efficacy of this exact Holistic House product as a validated treatment. No unsupported claim that a pattern, symptom, trauma memory or deeply held belief must change in 14 days.

## 2. Course passport — the whole arc

**Entry:** client explicitly consents to private saved progress; chooses topics or describes their concern; confirms five proposed questionnaires (editable); completes only missing eligible tests; views real scale results; explicitly chooses 3–5 practice scales; names one personally meaningful, observable challenge. Compatible historical results are reused.

**Format:** 14 calendar days according to the client’s time zone, approximately 5–12 minutes/day including optional 24h check-in, thought, 2-minute or standard guided practice, small action and short journal. One new cognitive/behavioral learning operation each day. The existing 84 individual bilingual lessons remain canonical for the six skill tracks.

**Personalization:** 3–5 selected scales still select the day’s topic; v2 additionally re-offers **one client-approved primary coping/attention skill** on all days as a *short optional anchor*. This preserves practice continuity despite the rotating scales. No clinical diagnosis, automatic deficits, or false causal attribution from questionnaire scores.

**What counts as learning:** a personally defined observation, one feasible response, a realistic if–then plan, one reflective note or choosing to rest. A recorded session means “I saved a practice”, not “I am cured” and not “the scale is normal”. Skipping a day's check-in leaves no fabricated score. The optional daily 0–10 difficulty is descriptive, not a validated psychometric measure.

**Review:** Day 7 = summary + adjust the load; Day 14 = summary + voluntary plan for the next 2–4 weeks and option to begin a new cycle. Continue/retest offers never promise automatic habit formation, and no reminders or repeated interventions are forced.

### Four phases

- **Days 1–3 · Notice the pattern:** goal, event/cue, thought/feeling/action and familiar inner rule.
- **Days 4–7 · Make room for new responding:** orientation/grounding, balanced thought or unhooking, low-stakes experiment, week-one review.
- **Days 8–11 · Take values-based action:** identify what matters, pair cue and response, compare expectation and experience, seek or offer support.
- **Days 12–14 · Consolidate and maintain:** create a toolkit with plan B, select tools independently, agree an optional continuation.

## 3. 14-day lesson passports

Each day below has **a distinctive workbook exercise**. The actual guided script, aphorism and small action still come from the already written lesson for the person's active track. Adaptation never replaces these 84 approved lessons with identical generic scripts.

| Day | Learning operation and underlying idea | Workbook question / action | Takeaway |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Define a workable, observable objective (values + behavioral planning) | “In which manageable situation would I like to react differently?” Name one small response within your control | My practical learning direction |
| **2** | See the everyday reaction chain (CBT ABC-style observation) | “Situation → thought or early sensation → what I did next” — write one ordinary example | A real personal cue and its response |
| **3** | Identify an old rule without declaring it false (CBT cognitive flexibility) | “Sometimes my mind says 'I must…'. What is this rule trying to protect? What price does it have?” | A rigid rule seen as a hypothesis |
| **4** | Restore choice with a safe pause (ACT/DBT-informed orientation) | “When I notice X, I can look at Y in my surroundings and decide whether to continue.” Natural breath; open eyes | One personally acceptable way to pause |
| **5** | Write a believable alternative, not magic affirmation (CBT/ACT) | “Evidence that fits / does not fully fit my thought; a kinder or more neutral possibility.” Alternatively: “I notice I am having this thought.” | One flexible working phrase |
| **6** | Predict and plan a **safe** behavioral experiment (CBT) | “I expect X; I could try tiny safe action Y; I will observe Z.” No confrontation, involuntary exposure or distress-provocation | One prediction and a tiny plan |
| **7** | Nonjudgmental week review (self-compassion + self-monitoring) | “What cue did I notice? What was usable, neutral or uncomfortable? Continue / softer / rest?” | A first-week adjustment |
| **8** | Act toward a freely chosen value (ACT committed action) | “I value kindness/honesty/clarity. One low-pressure step expressing it today is…” | An observable action connected to meaning |
| **9** | Attach one cue to one small response (if–then planning) | “If X happens, then I will try Y for 20–60 seconds.” Pick a cue that naturally recurs; never enforce | A practical context-linked prompt |
| **10** | Test the prediction against experience (behavioral learning) | “I expected X, I observed Y, and next time I could Z.” No pass/fail or causal claims | Evidence about one's actual response |
| **11** | Self-compassion and appropriate support (interpersonal skills) | “When interrupted, I can say ___ to myself, pause, or request help respectfully.” | A supportive phrase or accessible person/resource |
| **12** | Create a personal toolkit and plan B (skills consolidation + obstacle planning) | “My 2–3 chosen tools are… If obstacle X arises, my gentler backup is…” | A flexible toolkit, not one rigid routine |
| **13** | Autonomous contextual choice (generalization) | “Where could skill A fit? Where does rest, another skill or help fit better?” | Personal decision-making independent of the course |
| **14** | Descriptive end review + a maintenance plan (CBT self-management) | “What did I learn? Which cue and response will I voluntarily practise in the next 2–4 weeks?” | A personal continuation plan, not proof of cure |

### Model for a realistic new position (never a mandatory mantra)

**Old rule:** “Unless I do it perfectly, I will look foolish.”

**Balanced working position:** “I may still feel nervous, but I can try a small respectful step and learn from the response.”

**Tiny behavior:** Ask one ordinary clarifying question in a low-pressure context. *Optional, only if safe.*

**Workbook record:** “I expected ___; actually ___; one thing I can try next is ___.”

The goal is to help distinguish thought from fact and gain practical feedback, **not** to guarantee a deep rewrite of beliefs. For anxiety and distress the emphasis can be on tolerating and observing, not on forcing physiological changes.

## 4. Passport of the expanded lesson card (v2)

The 14 compact preview cards present: day number, status, approved domain-specific lesson title, common skill-learning operation, optional chosen scale, **skill of the day** and a concrete takeaway. Today has the visible burgundy action button; future lessons remain previews; older uncompleted days are labeled as earlier, not falsely marked completed. No clinical success or “health %” icon.

Expanded current day:

1. **Daily check-in (optional):** problem difficulty over the last 24 hours, 0–10; optional functioning/resources/note. Check-in can be skipped, corrected on the current day and remains separate from completing the session.
2. **Aphorism + understanding:** original lesson aphorism and domain-specific teaching; new **flexible working phrase**, clearly presented as an option rather than an affirmation to repeat until believed.
3. **Guided practice:** arrive safely → distinct track-specific exercise → return to the room; one 2-minute and one standard version; natural breathing, open eyes, text transcript, no imagery option.
4. **Tiny real-world experiment:** original personalized ritual PLUS one day-specific optional experiment and an expandable **familiar primary skill** that can be rehearsed for ~30–60 seconds; safe context and right to skip.
5. **Workbook + reflection:** a unique day question, optional first-person sentence starter, optional short (≤500-character) journal entry, feedback and immediate optional before/after state notes. The client presses **Save completed practice**; the existing server encrypts and saves the private note in an owner-scoped session. On reload the owner can read their saved note. No new unconsented data capture or browser storage.

**Completion rule:** A session may be marked saved whether or not the person answered the question; exercises are never forced, and “completed” signifies participation, not improved wellbeing. The day’s learning artefact is an invitation.

### What happens if a client had a bad experience?

- Existing session feedback `uncomfortable` suggests a gentler mode next day.
- Natural breathing, open eyes, and no-imagery path remain available.
- No pressure to provoke panic, test a trauma trigger, withdraw medication or use clinical exposure without professional support.
- The patient may pause, skip, seek real support or use a less demanding exercise.
- Do not auto-infer a diagnosis or “must improve” from rating scores.

### How to develop one skill despite 3–5 rotating scales

**Daily secondary focus:** determined from the same approved `coursePracticeFocus` for all 84 lessons and personalized future roadmap cards.

**Daily continuity:** `primaryGoal` from the voluntary, previously chosen goals offers a small optional, always familiar anchor for repetition. This addresses fragmentation without rewriting the client’s chosen scales or presuming a single clinical priority. Under `no_imagery`, repeat a neutral eyes-open orientation instead of a visualized scene. The primary anchor is a practice invitation, **not a new required task**.

## 7. Follow-on roadmap (not part of this patch)

Possible subsequent, independently consented enhancements: allow the client to name a preferred “primary skill” directly (instead of relying on first approved goal); explicit “skill use in real life” checkbox independent of 0–10 difficulty; editable if–then card in their private cabinet; printable reflection summary; optional check-in after 2–4 weeks. These require proper privacy/design review and must not be silently exposed as already implemented.

**Key product promise:** “14 days to learn, practise and begin applying a personally meaningful skill” — **never** “14 days to cure a condition” or “install a permanent subconscious belief”.
